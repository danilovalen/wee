# A what-if tool for adding and removing: diagnosis and proposal (2026-11-05)

Owner (register 755): *"I've been wanting for something to add/remove from the level that also reduces the
amount of moves. But then not always the least removal or most removal is good. Maybe the 'add or remove
something from somewhere to influence the level' should be something separate and a bit more robust, but
still easy to use."*

Diagnosis only, nothing changed.

## What exists today

| Tool | Where | What it tries | What it keeps |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Most moves** | tool panel, per tool | every empty tile x every way round (`placeBest`, `shape.js:45`) | the one that makes the answer **longest** |
| **Remove one** | Findings | every piece and every tile but the berry, taken out alone (`removeBest`, `shape.js:62`) | the one that makes the answer **longest** |
| **Trim** | Findings | rows and columns at the edges | the smallest room that plays the same |
| **Idle and bait notes** | Findings | each piece and wall taken out | the ones that change nothing (or only the traps) |

Three findings:

1. **The work is already done and then thrown away.** `placeBest` and `removeBest` solve the room once
   for every candidate and keep only the maximum. Every "shorter by 3", "same", or "unsolvable" result
   was computed and discarded. A what-if tool costs almost nothing extra to compute; the work is in
   showing the results.
2. **Each tool picks one extreme for you.** As the owner says, the longest is not always the best: a
   change can lengthen the answer by adding a dull detour, and a shortening can be what removes a cheap
   shortcut. Only the designer can judge, so the tool should show the choices, not choose.
3. **Adding and removing live in two places.** Most moves is in the tool panel and Remove one is in
   Findings, each with its own sentence and Apply. The idle and bait notes are a third view of the same
   question ("what if this piece were gone?").

## The proposal: a What if lens

One lens, in the header row with the others (749), that paints the answer to "what happens if I change this
tile" straight onto the room. It reads the tool in hand:

- **With a piece tool in hand (block, one-way, button...): what if I place it here?** Every empty tile is
  painted by what placing the piece there does, trying every way round and keeping the best way for each
  tile (its direction drawn as a small arrow).
- **With Erase in hand: what if I take this out?** Every piece and tile is painted by what removing it does.

What each tile shows:

| Result | Mark | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Longer | warm colour, and the change in moves | `+5` |
| Shorter | cool colour, and the change in moves | `−3` |
| Same answer | nothing (stays plain) | |
| Same length, but a different answer or other traps | a faint dot | |
| Breaks the room (no win) | a red X, as on the solution line (722) | |

Colour strength follows the size of the change, so the strongest choices stand out at a glance, and the
number makes it exact. The numbers follow 741 and 742: crisp, readable, never stacked.

Picking a choice is the same as everywhere else:

- **Focus a tile** (hover, Look tap or the room cursor, per 752): the detail line says it in full ("Place a
  one-way here, pointing up, and the answer takes 24 moves instead of 19, with 2 routes and 40 traps"), and
  the room previews it, lenses included (746 B).
- **Apply** (or A) makes it, as one undo step. Or just tap with the tool, as you would anyway: the lens
  only informs the normal edit.

Then:

- **Most moves and Remove one retire.** Their best answer is the brightest tile on the lens. A small
  "Longest" and "Shortest" jump on the lens's line can move the focus straight to either extreme.
- The idle notes in Findings stay (they are about the whole room); their tiles are the "same answer" tiles
  of the Erase view.

## Cost and how it stays fast

- One solve per candidate. A 10x10 room with ~60 empty tiles and a 4-way piece is ~240 solves; Remove is one
  per piece. That is the cost today's Most moves already pays, run in slices so nothing freezes (`run` in
  `shape.js`).
- The lens fills in as results arrive, tile by tile, and the previous result stays dimmed while it
  recomputes (720).
- Results hold until the room changes, and a change to the tool in hand (754) restarts them.
- A room too big to finish in a few seconds says so on the lens line and shows what it has.

## Later addition: padding is not length (register 756)

Owner: *"adding blocks to increase linear movement (no branching) is boring."* A change can add moves that are
all forced: each new step has one sensible move and adds no choice or trap. The lens should tell that apart
from real length, using the forced-steps and choices measures the room already has (708-710): such a tile's
`+5` is drawn hatched or dimmed, and its detail line says "5 more moves, all forced". To settle when building.
