The wee UI pass

Why the editor runs off the bottom of the screen, how 35 level editors, strategy games and creative tools keep a single screen readable, and what wee should take from them.

The root cause is the layout's shape, not the lens panel. The space under the room is the only place new things can go, and every feature since the one-screen fix went there: tool options, the hint, the key line, the live solve line, Show solution, design notes, and now four lens rows, a checkbox and up to four legend lines.

Each fits on its own. Together they push the room down to a third of the screen, the opposite of what an editor is for. The fit test stayed green because it never opened Lenses.

2. How other tools do it

Three research passes: puzzle and level editors, information-dense games, creative tools. The network policy blocked most direct page fetches, so most facts come from search summaries quoting official docs and wikis. Tiled, Lönn and PuzzleScript were read in full from their GitHub sources. Uncertain facts are marked.

Level editors

  • Super Mario Maker 2. Only the 12 most recent parts are on screen; hold one to pin it. The full catalogue opens as a grid. A part's options open in a bubble on the part itself and grey out the rest. Its director's stated goal was "the right number of icons onscreen"; reviews still call it crammed.
  • PuzzleScript. No option panels at all. Keys 1 to 0 pick tiles; a "+" on the room's edge adds a row or column, right click removes one; E switches between editing and playing.
  • Baba Is You. A bar of recent objects (right click locks one); typing a word picks that object up; the controls are listed along the bottom of the screen, so you learn them by seeing them.
  • Portal 2 Puzzle Maker. The item palette appears only when the mouse touches the left edge. Four buttons on top. Item settings open on right click, on the item. A debug overlay for connections sits on one key.
  • Lönn, the Celeste editor. Right click an object for its properties; double-click a material to make it a favourite; single keys rotate, flip and resize; every key can be rebound.
  • Tiled and LDtk. Tab hides every panel. One letter per tool. H opens a sheet of every shortcut (LDtk) or dims all but the current layer (Tiled).
  • A Sokoban research tool draws dead squares, the search and the best path on the grid, and pointing at a dead square says which rule kills it. The closest thing to wee's lenses, and it explains on hover, not in a panel.

Information-dense games

  • Europa Universalis, Hearts of Iron. Map modes are a small cluster on keys, and pressing a key again cycles to the next mode on that key. Hearts of Iron shows the common few and hides the rest behind one control.
  • Civilization VI. The settler lens opens by itself when a settler is selected. A popular mod made that a setting. The legend shows only while a lens is on (placement unconfirmed).
  • Cities: Skylines 2. Some heatmaps open by themselves, and players went looking for how to close them. An automatic overlay needs an obvious one-key dismiss.
  • Oxygen Not Included. About 15 overlays on F-keys, one at a time, with filters inside the overlay's legend.
  • Crusader Kings 3. Tooltips inside tooltips: detail arrives on hover instead of taking space. Critics put the useful depth at two or three levels.
  • Into the Breach. Danger is drawn on the tile itself; holding Alt numbers the order of events. Its lead's rule: "sacrifice cool ideas for the sake of clarity every time".
  • XCOM 2, Fire Emblem. Ranges are an outline of the area, not a fill of every tile. Fire Emblem shows every enemy's combined danger zone on one button.
  • RimWorld, Factorio. Stacking toggles and a single "show everything" key, both commonly called cluttered; Factorio lets you pick which layers stay on.
  • Lichess. Arrows by right-drag, colour by modifier key, one key toggles the engine's arrows, ? lists every shortcut.

Creative tools

  • Aseprite. The context bar, a strip under the menu, shows the current tool's options and nothing else. The timeline hides on Tab.
  • Photoshop, Photopea. Panels collapse to an icon strip; a "narrow options bar" turns the options' words into icons on small screens; Photopea folds each sidebar column into buttons that open one panel at a time.
  • Figma. The toolbar moved to the bottom to give the canvas room. Minimize UI folds both sides, and selecting something brings the properties back for a moment. Figma reversed its floating panels after launch because people who work for hours found them worse.
  • Blender. Overlays is one header button with two parts: a master on/off and a dropdown of grouped checkboxes that changes with the mode. The status bar shows the keys for what you are doing now.
  • tldraw. The contextual toolbar floats over the selection, shows only options that apply, hides while dragging, and is not drawn at all when nothing applies.
  • Ableton. The Info View: one small fixed box that explains whatever the mouse is over.
  • Linear, VS Code. A command palette that runs anything and shows each command's key, so using it teaches the shortcuts; every panel has a toggle; a mode that hides everything.

4. Proposal for wee

  1. 1 · Context bar. One line under the header, fixed height: the current tool's options, which sit under the room today, and its hint when it has none. It absorbs the options block, the place hint and the key line.
  2. 2 · Status bar. One line at the bottom, fixed: the live solve verdict, Show solution, a count of design notes that opens them, and the keys for what you are doing. In play: moves and the softlock warning.
  3. 3 · Design notes in a drawer, opened from the status bar, never inline. Their findings stay on the room as frames, as now.
  4. 4 · Lenses as four chips, one per row (Colour, Dots, Lines, Frames), on the room's edge. Each chip cycles its row on click and on its number key. The header's Lenses button turns them all on or off and holds Follow the tool. The legend becomes one short line per lit chip, so it is never longer than four. A lens the tool opened looks hollow until you pick one yourself.
  5. 5 · Hover a tile, read everything. One info line in the status bar: "3 moves to win · a laser reaches here · you only pass". Long legend words move there too.
  6. 6 · Right column as tabs: Room, Powers, World. Or the room's size moves to "+" handles on its edges, which frees the column for powers.
  7. 7 · Narrow windows. Tool labels become icons below about 1100 px wide, with names and keys in the tooltip; the right column folds to an icon rail.
  8. 8 · Tab hides both side columns; ? lists every key; each tool button shows its key.
  9. 9 · The gate. The fit test opens everything at once (every lens row, design notes, the tool with the most options, play mode) at every size it tests, and asserts nothing is cut and the room keeps at least 60% of the height. It fails today by 167 px, which is the point.
