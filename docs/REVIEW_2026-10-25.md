## Architecture: proposals, biggest payoff first

1. **One replay per path.** Each goal replays the robot's path on its own: 44 `replayPath` calls across the goal modules and the search scores, so a level with 3 goals and 24 shortest paths replays ~72 times per Run, and the search does that for every room it tries. Record one trace per path (each tick's robot, pieces, tiles, beams, events) and let every goal read it. Each goal would also define one `measure` from 0 to 1, with met = 1, which retires `tools/searchScores.mjs`: today every goal is written twice, once as a yes/no judge and once as partial credit, and the two can disagree. **Done in part (462, step 3):** each path is replayed once per room and its ticks are shared by every goal (`replayPath` keeps them): Run's checks over every known room take 22% less time and the search makes 27% more climbs in the same time. **The `measure` half is dropped:** reading `miss()`, a search score is only consulted after the goal's judge said missed, and it is capped below 1, so it guides the climb and never decides a goal; folding it into the goal would mix a rule with a search heuristic.
2. **Pushable tiles as pieces.** A pushable one-way, door or relay lives in the cells and moves by rewriting them. That is why spikes need a second record (`s.spikes`) to come back, why goals must follow tiles through push events (`tileLog`), and why the box rules (`pushTo`, `pushChain`, the box-on-a-one-way case) have three paths for one push. As pieces that carry a tile kind, the cells would hold only the fixed floor: spikes stay spikes underneath, a piece is tracked by its id like a box, and one push rule covers boxes, lasers and tiles. Biggest change here; it touches levels 19, 20, 22, 23 and the Sandbox. **He said yes (461). First step built:** each pushable tile is a piece with an id (`s.tiles`), and the ground under it is kept (`s.ground`), so spikes come back without a second record and goals follow a tile by its id. Still open: the push itself has separate paths for tiles (`pushChain`) and pieces (`pushTo`, `train`), and `s.cells` still shows the tiles over the ground for the rules that read it.
3. **One room legend.** Rooms are written as text in at least four legends that disagree: `X` is spikes in `tests/lib.mjs` but a red-and-blue door in the campaign tests and the room search; `x` is spikes in the room search. One legend module, used by tests, tools and the known rooms. **Done (462, step 1):** `tools/legend.mjs`; the Sandbox tests moved onto the campaign letters (Sandbox-only pieces took free letters: `Z` enemy, `A` strong enemy, `~` water), 165 room strings converted from a log of the rooms each test drew; a test reads and prints every known room back unchanged.
4. **A committed level-measuring tool.** Levels 21 to 24 were measured with one-off scratch scripts (search, keep the smallest room per move count, add pieces to known wins, fix a plan's core and search the rest). `tools/measureLevel.mjs` would make those runs repeatable and recordable. **Done (462, step 2):** `node tools/measureLevel.mjs <level> [--seconds S] [--runs N] [--add goals] [--require goals] [--steps min,max] [--palette ...] [--seeded] [--from rows]` runs climbs in parallel and prints wins by move count with the smallest room for each, plus a JSON line; the level options are shared with `tools/levelSearch.mjs` (`tools/levelDef.mjs`).
5. **Split the two files at the 300-line cap.** `src/app/campaign.js` (299) and `src/app/rooms.js` (299) each hold three screens' worth of work; comments are being cut to fit. Campaign splits into the level list, the goal card and run/progress. **Done (462, step 5):** `campaign.js` 299 → 182 lines (`progress.js` holds saved progress, `goalCard.js` the goal card and verdict); `rooms.js` 299 → 232 (`roomList.js` holds the saved-rooms list). On the way a moved storage key went missing and the save's own try/catch hid it; the browser tests caught it.
6. **Group the goal modules by subject**, after 1: `extras.js`, `oneWay.js`, `spikes.js`, `tiles.js` and `laserGoals.js` are grouped by when they were added. **Done (462, step 4):** `src/campaign/goals/` holds doors, switches, boxes, one-ways, lasers and spikes, with the shared tracking in `helpers.js` (four copies of the direction table became one); `requirements.js` only joins them and draws a trail.
