**Write what a person would say out loud.** (2026-10-30: *"Too weird wording, intransitive, uncommon
verbs should be avoided"*, then *"'run moves the robot for you' is useless bad writing"*, with his own
example: *"By tapping Run, the robot will find the shortest path to its goal and walk through it."*)
Plain, common words in a natural order. No rare verb ("steer"), no vague "then it plays", and no
sentence bent to satisfy a rule: "Run moves the robot for you" followed "use a verb with an object"
and still read badly.

**Teaching text is an order; a goal list can stay short.** (2026-10-30: *"The goals themselves can
be shorter. X into Y. Problem is the tutorial. Where you are teaching the game, not listing goals."*)
In a list of goals, "Box into a closed door" is fine. Where the text teaches the game, a goal's short
form reads as something already happening: the tutorial's "Robot reaches the star" should say "Get the
robot to the star."
