Progression: five sketches (register 588)

One campaign holding three kinds of level: BUILD today's levels, PUZZLE you drive the robot, CHALLENGE place given pieces in a built room. Stars are never spent: a star total only ever opens things (skins, palettes, tools, level packs). Each sketch is a different answer to "what does the player see between levels". Words are drafts.

A. The map

wee★ 23

From Baba Is You and Super Mario World's overworld.

  • Levels are nodes on a map you walk the robot across (the robot slides on the map too, the way it does in rooms).
  • Beating a level opens the paths next to it; a world's gate opens at a star total.
  • A dotted side path appears only when a side star is earned: the side stars find secrets.
  • Kinds mix freely along the way; a new mechanic gets its own region.
Good: exploration, places to remember, secrets. Costs: map art per world, and choices can strand a player who skips ahead.

B. The track

wee★ 23

From Duolingo's path, and Cut the Rope's level boxes.

  • One column of levels, always one "now" node: there is never a choice of where to go next.
  • Units of 6 to 10 levels, each about one mechanic, each mixing build, puzzle and challenge so the mechanic is seen three ways.
  • A gift node every few levels opens a skin or a palette; a unit's last node needs a star total from that unit.
  • Side stars show as small stars beside a beaten node; tap it to go back for them.
Good: simplest to read, best on a phone, no art. Costs: no exploration; it feels like a course.

C. Hub rooms

wee★ 23

From Snakebird, Stephen's Sausage Roll and Patrick's Parabox hubs.

  • The menu is a room: you slide the robot into a door to enter a set.
  • A door is a level kind or a mechanic; a locked door shows the star total that opens it.
  • The wardrobe corner holds skins and palettes already earned; tools appear on the hub floor when opened.
  • The hub itself can be a tiny puzzle, so getting around teaches sliding.
Good: uses the game's own rules as its menu, very "wee". Costs: slower to reach a level than a list; must stay readable on a phone.

D. Packs and a reward shelf

wee★ 23

From Angry Birds and Cut the Rope episodes, and a battle-pass style milestone line (with nothing to buy).

  • The level list grows into packs, one per kind or mechanic; each shows its stars out of the total.
  • One reward line counts every star; each milestone opens one thing, shown before it is reached so there is always a next goal.
  • Closest to what exists now: the list, the star totals and the cards are all built.
Good: cheapest to build, clear goals. Costs: the least surprise; rewards are announced, never found.

E. Discovered by knowing, not by stars

wee★ 23

From A Monster's Expedition and The Witness: what you have learned opens the next place.

  • A grid by mechanic (rows) and kind (columns): beating the build level of a mechanic reveals its puzzle, then its challenge.
  • Stars do not open levels at all; they only open the cosmetic and tool rewards, so a stuck player can always move on by learning something else.
  • Hidden tiles keep discovery without a map.
Good: no star wall ever blocks play; each mechanic is seen three ways in order. Costs: a grid is less charming than a map; needs every mechanic in all three kinds.

Unlock everything, this session only (register 589)

  • Where: a small open-padlock icon in the main menu's top-right corner, the size of the other corner icons.
  • What it does: one tap opens every level, pack, reward, skin, palette and tool, and the hidden levels (the two-colour ones). Tap again to put it back.
  • Session only: kept in memory, never written to saved progress, so closing or reloading the page ends it. Stars earned while it is on still count, since they are real wins.
  • While on: a thin padlock badge sits beside the star total on every screen, so a screenshot never passes for real progress.
  • Gate: a test turns it on, checks a locked thing opens, reloads, and checks saved progress is unchanged and the lock is back.
